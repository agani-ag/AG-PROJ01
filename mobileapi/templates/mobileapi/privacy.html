1. Overview

SyncUp is a web browser with optional SyncUp accounts. You can browse without an account. Accounts are either created for you by an administrator or a partner service, or created by you in the app (when sign-up is available). This policy explains what data the app handles, why, and your choices.

2. Information we collect

Account information (you provide, only if you sign in). Your name and an email address and/or mobile number, and optionally a username you choose — used to sign you in and identify your account. Your password is sent over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection and stored by our backend only as a secure hash. On your device, your session token and optional app-lock PIN are kept in encrypted storage.

Browser sync (only while you're signed in with Sync on). Your bookmarks, your Normal-tab browsing history (kept for 90 days), your new-tab shortcuts, a few browser settings (search engine, pop-up blocking, theme) and the titles and addresses of your open Normal tabs are stored on our servers so they appear on your other signed-in devices. They are used only for that. SyncUp staff do not view them — our administrators see only counts (for example, how many bookmarks are synced) and when each device last synced. Passwords, cookies, SyncUp tabs (links SyncUp provides), Incognito tabs, downloaded files and site permissions are never synced. You can turn Sync (or any part of it) off, or delete your synced data, in Settings → Sync. Browsing while signed out stays on your device.

Tools that sync (only while you're signed in with Sync on). Your Calendar to-dos and lists, your Shopping lists, and your Wellbeing records — water you drank, daily step counts, habits and their ticks, sleep times and mood check-ins — are stored on our servers so they appear on your other signed-in devices. They are used only for that; administrators see counts, never their contents (mood check-ins are never shown to administrators at all). Each can be turned off in Settings → Sync; signed out, they stay on your phone. Everything else in Tools — Forest, Breaks, Decide, Split bill, Sensors, Music, Videos — stays on your phone, apart from the test data the Sensors Network test exchanges with its test server (see Sharing and third parties).

Device information (automatic, for every install). A per-installation identifier, a Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) push token, the app version, Android version, device manufacturer and model, WebView version, language, time zone, the store the app was installed from, whether notifications are allowed, and your country (worked out from the connection — we do not store your IP address). Used to deliver notifications, support the app and understand which devices and versions are in use. We do not collect advertising IDs, hardware identifiers (such as IMEI), your location, or the list of apps on your device. On the sign-in screen you can pick your number from Google's own phone-number list; only the number you tap is filled in, and SyncUp never reads it from the device itself.

Partner services. If a partner service (for example your accounting provider) added you to SyncUp, we keep that connection and the partner's own reference for you. A partner can send you links, notices and verification prompts only after you enable it with the password it gave you, and you can turn it off at any time.

Camera, microphone, location and physical activity (only when a feature needs them). Android asks you before any of these is used, and you can say no. None of them is sent to or stored on our servers.

  • Camera: the Sensors tools that look through the camera (for example Magnifier and Colour picker). The picture is used on your phone while the tool is open and is not saved or sent anywhere.
  • Microphone: Walkie Talkie (in a channel or a SyncUp Group) sends your voice only while you talk, straight to the nearby phones you chose — never to our servers, and it is not recorded. The Sensors sound meter measures loudness on your phone.
  • Location: the Sensors tools that need GPS (speed, height, GPS status) and, on older Android versions, finding nearby phones for SyncUp Share and SyncUp Group. Used on your phone only.
  • Physical activity: Steps in Wellbeing counts your steps on your phone; the daily total syncs to your account only if you're signed in with Wellbeing sync on.
  • Pages you open in the app may ask for your camera, microphone or location too; that goes to the page, at the moment it asks.

Nearby features (SyncUp Share, SyncUp Group, Party, Walkie Talkie). Files, photos, songs, videos and voice you share with phones near you travel directly between the phones over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi. They never pass through or stay on our servers. Photos shared in a group are kept on each phone only until the group ends, unless someone saves them.

Files and photos you choose. When a page asks you to upload a file or take a photo, or you add photos to SyncUp Share or a group, only what you explicitly select (through Android's own picker) or capture is used. SyncUp does not scan your photos or files. Music lists the songs on your phone to play them; they are never uploaded.

3. How we use information

  • To authenticate you and provide the app's features, including syncing your browser data between your own devices.
  • To send you notifications and reminders you are configured to receive, and announcements from SyncUp (you can turn these off in Settings → Notifications → SyncUp updates).
  • To maintain security (e.g., the optional app-lock, limits on sign-in attempts) and to operate and support the app, using counts and device details — not the contents of your browsing.

We do not sell your personal information and do not use it for advertising.

4. Sharing and third parties

  • Google Firebase (Cloud Messaging and Remote Config) delivers push notifications and configuration — Google Privacy Policy.
  • Partner services you have enabled receive only what they need to reach you (for example, whether a verification prompt was answered). They never receive your synced browser data.
  • Websites your administrator switched on (SyncUp links and shortcuts) receive a random code for you, different for each website, so that website can send you notifications through SyncUp. It does not include your name, email or phone number.
  • SyncHub, our own website of tools that opens inside SyncUp, gets a copy of your SyncUp account so the same account works there: your name, email, phone number, your password in scrambled (hashed) form, and SyncHub's random code for you. Partner users are left out unless their administrator turns General links on for them. It only goes from SyncUp to SyncHub, never back.
  • Web content you open in the app may be operated by us or third parties; any camera/microphone/location/file access you grant goes to the page you opened, under that page's own terms.
  • Cloudflare runs the test server for the Sensors Network test. Only when you press Start, your phone exchanges test data with it to measure your connection, so Cloudflare sees your IP address, as any website does. The test data says nothing about you, and your results stay on your phone — Cloudflare Privacy Policy.
  • A DNS service you turn on in Settings → Browser → DNS looks up the names of the sites you open in SyncUp, including in Incognito tabs, so that service can see those names. All of them are off until you choose one. We don't record which you use, and a DNS you add yourself stays on your phone.
  • We may disclose information if required by law.

5. Data security

  • Communication between the app and SyncUp's servers uses HTTPS (encrypted in transit). Web pages you choose to open may use plain HTTP, like in any browser.
  • Session tokens and the app-lock PIN are held in Android's encrypted storage.
  • Sign-in tokens expire automatically and can be revoked; you or an administrator can sign a device out.

6. Data retention and deletion

  • Account data is retained while your account is active. Synced history older than 90 days is removed automatically.
  • Delete your synced data: Settings → Sync → Delete synced data erases it from our servers (each device keeps its own copy until you clear it there).
  • Delete your account in the app: open Settings → Delete account. Your account is deleted permanently, straight away, and cannot be restored: we erase your profile (name, email, phone, password), everything synced to it (bookmarks, history, open tabs, settings, to-dos, shopping lists and wellbeing data), your links, reminders, support chat, partner connections, page tokens and the notifications sent to you. You are signed out on every device. Data already on your phones stays there until you clear it or uninstall the app.
  • Installs nobody has signed in on are removed from our records after 6 months without use.
  • You may also request deletion by contacting us at the address below.
  • Uninstalling the app removes locally stored data (browsing data, session token, PIN, cached reminders) from your device.

7. Children

SyncUp is intended for business use and is not directed to children under 13 (or the minimum age in your jurisdiction).

8. Permissions summary

  • Camera — the Sensors tools that use the camera, and in-app pages that ask. Never sent to us.
  • Microphone — Walkie Talkie (to the nearby phones you chose) and the Sensors sound meter, and in-app pages that ask. Never sent to us or recorded.
  • Location — the Sensors GPS tools, finding nearby phones on older Android versions, and in-app pages that ask. Used on your phone; not collected by us.
  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth, Wi-Fi) — SyncUp Share, SyncUp Group, Party and Walkie Talkie, to connect to phones near you.
  • Physical activity — Steps in Wellbeing.
  • Music and audio — only when you open Music, to list and play the songs on your phone. Your songs stay on your device; we never upload or collect them.
  • Photos and videos — no permission: you pick them with Android's own picker. On Android 9 and older, storage permission is asked only to save downloads and photos.
  • Notifications — to show push notifications and reminders.
  • Fingerprint / face — only if you turn on the app lock with biometrics.
  • Running in the background — to keep music, transfers, downloads and Walkie Talkie going while the screen is off; a notification shows while they do.
  • Run after reboot — to restore your scheduled reminders after a restart.
